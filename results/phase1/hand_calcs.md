## 2. What beam temperature can push the girder 6.25 in. sideways at column 79?

The beams run east-west; the girder is skewed 14.6° from the beam-normal direction (A05), so a westward beam displacement u moves the girder node u cos(theta) perpendicular to the girder and u sin(theta) along it (toward column 79). The beam nearest column 79 meets the girder at 467 in. from column 44 (A04).

Upper bound on the girder end's lateral movement: all of the nearest beam's free elongation goes west (exterior end fully held, no slip), and the girder rotates as a rigid bar about column 44, which amplifies the movement at column 79 by L / s = 540 / 467 = 1.157. Losses that reduce it: the clearance the beam's exterior end must close before it bears on its column (0.5 in., A27) plus seat-bolt slip.

| Case | Lateral movement needed at col 79 | Beam elongation needed | Beam T, NIST alpha | Beam T, EC3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rigid pivot about col 44, no losses | 6.25 in. | 5.58 in. | 659 | 632 |
| Girder translates with the beam (no pivot), no losses | 6.25 in. | 6.46 in. | 759 | 713 |
| Rigid pivot, 0.5 in. exterior clearance lost | 6.25 in. | 6.08 in. | 716 | 679 |
| Translation, 0.5 in. exterior clearance lost | 6.25 in. | 6.96 in. | 817 | 868 |

With the pre-errata seat (5.5 in. lateral), the rigid-pivot, no-loss case needs 582 °C (NIST alpha).

Reading: on beam elongation alone, lateral walk-off at column 79 needs beam temperatures of roughly 632 °C to 868 °C, above the 400 °C at which NIST says expansion damage occurred and at or above the 600 °C end of NIST's partial-floor ramp. Things that could reduce it and must come from the models: girder bending in plan between beams, column 79 moving east (16-story model, §11.4.1), and rotation or rocking of the girder on its seat (the mechanism in Table 8-2). This bound is what the beam-element model is checked against.

## 3. Force to restrain expansion against connection capacity

Q = A E(T) eps_th (NCSTAR 1-9 §8.7.2 Eq. 2). Bolt group capacities: 4 bolts x 45 kip = 180 kip at each girder seat; 6 x 45 = 270 kip at each fin (A07, A11).

- Girder fully restrained: bolts at a seat reach 180 kip after ΔT = 180 / (38.3 x 29000 x 1.4e-5) = **12 °C**.
- With hole clearance and bolt deformation at one end (0.41 in., A24, A25, A31) taken up first: ΔT ≈ 0.41 / (540 x 1.4e-5) + 12 = **65 °C**, i.e. girder about 85 °C. NIST Table 8-2: seat bolts at col 79 failed at girder 131 °C.
- Floor beam fully restrained: fin bolts reach 270 kip after ΔT = 41 °C.
- NIST's 600 °C example: Q = 38.3 x 22,235 x 1.4e-5 x 580 = 6915 kip (report: about 6900 kip).

## 4. Shear studs on the floor beams

Eq. 5 of §8.7.4 with the force shared equally by all 28 studs (A06) and slab fully restrained: studs reach 19.5 kip (A13) at ΔT = 19.5 x 28 / (16.2 x 29000 x 1.4e-5) = **83 °C** (beam 103 °C). NIST Table 8-2: first stud failure at beam 103 °C. Because the end studs carry more than the average, the first failure in a model should come somewhat earlier than this.

Girder lateral stiffness over beam axial stiffness (§8.7.4 Eq. 4): 48 Iy L1 / (A L2^3) = 2.56e-03 (report: 2.6e-3). The girder by itself barely resists the beams' push.

## 5. Buckling of the floor beams and the girder in plan (20 °C, scale by E(T)/E(20) when hot)

- W24x55, weak-axis flexural buckling over 624 in. with no bracing: pi^2 E Iy / L^2 = **21 kip**.
- W24x55 with its top flange held by the slab (restrained-axis torsional buckling, a = 11.5 in.): (GJ + pi^2 E (Cw + Iy a^2) / L^2) / (rx^2 + ry^2 + a^2) = **87 kip**.
- W33x130 girder, weak axis over 540 in.: 214 kip; between beams (longest segment 112 in.): 2053 kip (capped at squash load).
- E(600 °C)/E(20 °C) = 0.75 (NIST Eq. 7).

Once the studs fail, a beam's resistance to buckling in plan depends almost entirely on what holds its top flange (slab contact and friction). This is why S15 is swept.

## 8. Descent kinematics check (for Phase 3)

Free fall over the 242 ft that the north-face roofline dropped (NCSTAR 1-9 §12.5.3): t = sqrt(2h/g) = **3.88 s** (report 3.9 s; observed 5.4 s). Free fall over stage 2 (1.75 s to 4.0 s) from rest covers 82 ft; NIST reports about 105 ft (8 storeys) for that stage (NCSTAR 1A §3.6), which implies the roofline was already moving at about 10 ft/s at 1.75 s. These are the targets the Phase 3 roofline history is compared with.

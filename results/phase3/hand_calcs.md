## 2. Can intact tenant floors bridge a removed segment of Column 79?

At each typical tenant floor (`87.5 psf` over `~1,950 ft²`), Column 79 supports **170.6 kip/floor** statically, or **341.2 kip/floor** dynamically under sudden column removal (`DAF = 2.0`).

- **Vierendeel / shear-tab bending:** The seated (`STC`) connection on `A2001` and shear connections on `A2002` and `W36x135` transfer at most **15.5 kip/floor** across 29 to 45.6 ft spans—under **8 %** of static demand.
- **Large-deflection catenary action:** Combined horizontal tensile capacity of the north `STC` bolts (180 kip), south knife connection (~200 kip), west header (~260 kip), and slab `WWF` mesh (~127 kip) is **767 kip**. By the time the shortest span (`29 ft` west girder) reaches ~0.9 in. of axial connection tear-out slip, vertical deflection is **25.0 in.**, and even if all four paths peaked together they would carry only **45.3 kip/floor** (27 % of static, 13 % of sudden-removal demand). Bridging one floor statically at that sag would require **3,122 kip** of horizontal tension (**6,243 kip** dynamically).

**Reading:** Simple/seated tenant floors above a removed Column 79 segment cannot bridge the double span in either bending or catenary action unless the perimeter/hat structure above redistributes the entire column load—which WTC 7 lacked (no roof hat truss; east penthouse posts sat directly on Columns 76, 77, 79, 80 and roof beams).

## 3. Falling-floor cascade energy check

One detached `1,950 ft²` floor bay (`170.6 kip`) falling one storey (`153 in.`) releases **26,106 kip-in.** of gravitational potential energy.

- Direct shear-bolt fracture work around the receiving bay (~32 bolts x 45 kip over ~0.5 in.): **540 kip-in.**
- Upper-bound beam plastic-hinge + connection work before `~0.06 rad` end rotation tears out shear tabs (net of the receiving bay's own static load work): **2,859 kip-in.**
- **Single-floor overload ratio:** `26,106 / 2,859` = **9.1x** more impact energy than an intact tenant bay can absorb.

**Key insight:** Column 79 had `26 x 2 in.` *flange-tip side cover plates* (`I_min = 14,724 in⁴`), so it needed **5 to 6 unbraced storeys** to buckle under gravity alone. By contrast, neighbouring **Columns 80, 76, and 81** used *web plates* between the flanges (`I_min = 4,727 to 4,791 in⁴`—less than one-third of Column 79's weak-axis inertia!), and **Columns 77 and 78** were bare `W14x665` (`I_min = 4,170 in⁴`). Consequently, **Columns 80, 81, 76, 77, and 78 all lose stability at just 3 to 4 unbraced storeys** under their own baseline gravity loads—before even counting load shed from Column 79!

## 5. Transfer Truss 2 check (Floors 5 to 7) under loss of Column 80

Truss 2 spans between Column 74 (west) and Column 80 (east) from Floor 5 to Floor 7 (`~27 ft` depth), supporting **Column 77** (`3,346 kip`) and half of transfer girder `MG-23` carrying **Column 78** (`~1,705 kip`) = **5,050 kip** total:

- **Intact state:** East reaction onto Column 80 below Floor 5 is **2,479 kip**; compression in the east diagonal (`W14x730 + 2 PL 8x3`) is **3,572 kip** against AISC capacity **5,873 kip** (safe while Column 80 supports the east end).
- **If Column 80 fails or buckles in the double-height Floor 6 mechanical space:** Truss 2 must cantilever `5,050 kip` at `27 ft` from Column 74 (`M = 136,364 kip-ft`), demanding **5,050 kip** of pure tension across the Floor 7 top-chord tie into Column 74 (roughly **3x** a heavy bolted/welded chord connection's `~1,600.0 kip` capacity).

**Reading:** Losing Column 80 directly undermines the east support of Truss 2, providing a clear structural pathway to destabilize Core Columns 77 and 78 (`Group 2`) even if upper-floor shear connections have already detached.

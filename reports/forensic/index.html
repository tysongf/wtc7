WTC 7 floor 13 girder at column 79: Phase 1 forensic report

Interim report, built 2026-11-02T21:17Z from the repository state. Study brief dated 2026-10-21. Every number on this page is read from the run outputs, assumption table and checksum files at build time (scripts/build_report.py).

Interim. The Phase 1 gate has not passed, and nothing here is a finding about why WTC 7 collapsed. Detailed-model runs completed: 65 of 65 listed (0 running). The outcome class is stated (Section 6); it differs from NIST's, and the NIST-replica runs did not find what explains the difference (Section 7).

1. Summary

The question for Phase 1 is whether heating of the floor 13 framing in the northeast corner of WTC 7, as NIST described it, makes the W33x130 girder spanning from column 79 to column 44 (erection mark A2001) lose its seat at column 79. NIST's report (NCSTAR 1-9) identifies that event as the start of the collapse. This report covers the evidence gathered so far, the models built and what their completed runs show.

  1. The fabrication drawings change the geometry at column 79. Frankel shop drawings 9114 and 1091 and erection drawing E12/13 (NIST FOIA releases) show the girder meeting column 79's north face only 4.17 deg off square (written on the sheets; the column's 12.3 deg rotation from the grid is inferred from three members), not at the 14.6 deg this study had digitized from NCSTAR 1-9 Fig. 8-16. They also show the girder skew is 16.5 deg, and that the girder end sits at the mouth of a recess formed by the column's 26 in. side cover plates, with about 4.15 in. of room to the west plate once inside it (Section 3).
  2. The study's first screening result is withdrawn. On the digitized geometry the screening model found walk-off decided by friction at the column 79 face, switching between 0.22 and 0.24. That threshold came from the wrong face angle (Section 5.1).
  3. On the drawn geometry the screening model shows no walk-off for any friction from 0 to 0.3, for the girder end 0.4 in. nearer or further, or with heating then cooling. The heated girder pushes its end about 1.5 in. into the recess; friction or the west side plate then stops it at 1.6 to 4.0 in. of westward movement, short of the 6.25 in. needed. Walk-off occurs only with zero friction and the side plates removed (Section 5.2).
  4. In the detailed shell model no run walks off under NIST's schedule (600 C beams, 500 C girder, held 1.4 s): not the baseline and not any one-at-a-time value of the preregistered sweeps. In the baseline the girder end at column 79 moves at most 1.47 in. west, against 6.25 in. for walk-off. The reported runs heat at half NIST's rate, because the full rate failed the preregistered rate check (Sections 6 and 8).
  5. One run walks off: the preregistered most-favourable combination, at 700 C during the hold, after the girder buckles; a repeat reproduces it and continues to loss of support. The Section 6 repeat, with four ends replaced by the values the sweeps showed move the girder further, does not walk off. The outcome class is CONDITIONAL, on the combination as a whole: no single parameter flips the result (Section 6).
  6. The model departs from NIST at beam buckling. The connection events agree with NIST's within 40 C through column 79 (first stud 101 C, all column 79 bolts 154 C; NIST 103 and 194 C), but no floor beam buckles in the baseline, where NIST's northmost beam began to buckle at 436 C and all of them by 600 C. The beams' compression peaks at 117 kip; the expanding beams push the girder sideways instead (it bows 4.3 in.). What explains the difference is not yet found; a run with NIST's idealizations at column 79 is under way (Section 7).
  7. Checks. The shell solver written for this study reproduces closed-form results (thermal expansion, restrained force, Euler buckling, friction thresholds, bolt failure) and matches CalculiX within 1.6 % for lateral-torsional buckling. Two preregistered checks fail: mesh, narrowly (Section 8), and quasi-static, because kinetic energy exceeds 5 % of internal energy during the gravity ramp in every run and briefly during heating in most. Neither can change an outcome, but the Phase 1 gate has not passed.

2. The question and NIST's account

NIST's leading hypothesis (NCSTAR 1-9, Chapters 8, 11 and 12; FAQ) is that fire on floors 8 to 13 heated the floor beams east of column 79, whose thermal expansion pushed girder A2001 laterally at column 79 after its erection bolts had sheared, until it lost its seat on floor 13. The floors below then failed in a cascade to floor 5, leaving column 79 unsupported over nine storeys; it buckled, followed by the east penthouse, columns 80 and 81 and the rest of the core.

NIST tested the floor framing mechanism in a partial-floor LS-DYNA model (§8.8): shells about 1.8 in., one layer of slab shells, bolts and studs as springs, gravity ramped over 1 s, then the beams heated uniformly to 600 C and the girder to 500 C between 1.1 and 2.6 s. Its reported sequence (Table 8-2):

The June 2012 errata set the column 79 seat width at 12 in. (from 11) and the lateral walk-off distance at 6.25 in. (from 5.5). The 16-story ANSYS model (§11.3) found walk-off at column 79 on floor 13 between 3.7 and 4.0 h with floor 13 steel at 300 to 700 C. NIST did not release its model input files; this study rebuilds the models from the published reports and drawings.

3.3 Secondary sources

The 2020 University of Alaska Fairbanks report (Hulsey, Quan, Xiao), AE911Truth's 2020 request for correction and NIST's 2020 reply discuss the side plates. The first two argue that the heated girder becomes trapped behind the west side plate; NIST's reply describes the seat as attached to the exterior edges of the side plates with the girder at a slight angle. On the girder's room-temperature position these sources agree with the reading above within its uncertainty. None of them was used as an input; they are summarized in sources/drawings/READING.md.

4.3 Solver verification

The shell solver passes 22 unit tests against closed-form results (tests/test_xfe.py): material curves within 1 % of the NIST and EN 1993-1-2 tables, free thermal expansion within 0.4 % (preregistered limit 1 %), restrained thermal force exact while elastic, a heated pinned strut's buckling load within 2 % of Euler, a block on an incline sticking or sliding exactly at tan(theta) = mu, a plate pushed into a skewed face sliding only when friction is below tan(14.6 deg), bolt springs breaking at the temperature-reduced capacity, and contact carrying an applied load exactly. Gravity check. Under gravity alone the support reactions sum to 216.0 kip against 215.6 kip applied. Two modelling choices were corrected during gravity-only pilots, before any heated run: an elastic slab and rigid top-plate ties clamped the beam ends against their seats, so the slab now follows NIST's tension/compression model (A72) and the clip-angle and top-plate ties have the bending stiffness of those parts (A80). A residual prying pattern remains: each beam end rotates on the toe of its seat and the seat carries about twice the simple end shear, limited by the cracked slab's local strength.

6. Detailed shell model results

Outcome class (preregistration Section 3): CONDITIONAL. No run reaches E2, E2r or E2s under NIST's 600 C schedule. The preregistered most-favourable combination (favourable: the Section 6 ends, column 79 0.4 in. further away, peak 700 C) walks off at 700 C during the hold: the girder buckles, its end twists past atan(bf/d) and moves 6.25 in. west, and the solver then diverges 0.008 s after E2. Its repeat with state dumps (favourable_dump, a diagnostic) reproduces the sequence within 0.07 s and continues to loss of support without diverging. The Section 6 repeat (favourable_s6: standard holes, friction 0.1, the drawn column 44 gap and 0.25 in. bolt deformation, the values that each moved the girder end furthest) does not walk off, and its girder does not buckle. No single parameter moves the result across the 700 C line, so the condition is the combination as a whole. Full account: docs/writeup.md Sections 4 and 5.

The holes change nothing. The drawn face slows the sliding (about 1 in. per 100 C against 1.6 at 14.6 deg) and delays walk-off past 600 C, but not past 700 C. Friction 0.3 stops the sliding, but the girder then buckles sideways and twists its end off at 650 to 655 C, in a dynamic phase where kinetic energy is up to a third of internal energy. With the drawn face and the side plates, the end bears on the west plate from 488 C and stays there to 700 C while the girder buckles. Rate. Halving the heating rate moved T_E3 by about 410 C, so under Section 9.4 every priority-1 run was repeated at half rate and the half-rate runs are the reported ones; priority 2 and the follow-up runs were made at half rate from the start. Half and quarter rate agree, so half rate is converged. The large move is not a smooth rate effect: the two girder-end bolt groups fail within about 20 C of each other, and at full rate the floor is still carrying the gravity transient when the race is decided.

Mesh. The 3.6 against 1.8 in. pair failed, so the 0.9 in. run decides (Section 9.4). It passes on T_E1 and fails on the lateral movement by a few tenths of a percentage point: 0.034 in., against the 6.25 in. that walk-off needs. Its hourglass energy was about 0.22 of internal energy, so the finer mesh is not clearly the more accurate one. Reported as a failure.

Quasi-static. The large ratios come from NIST's 1 s gravity ramp, before any heating; during heating they are short excursions at connection failures and buckling. Reported as a failure, not waived; the evidence that the results are not rate effects is the half-against-quarter-rate agreement.

9. Limitations

  • Phase 1 uses NIST's uniform temperature ramp, not its computed fire temperatures; that is Phase 2.
  • The girder's piece drawing is missing; its column 44 end clearance and cut-back are assumed (A96).
  • As-built fit-up is unknown; the drawn girder end has about 0.4 in. of margin to the side-plate recess (S11 sweeps ±0.4 in.).
  • Seats, top plates and column faces are rigid planes and springs, not meshed plates; column 79 is fixed except in S5.
  • Camber, the W12x26 framing in from the west and the slab's thermal response are not modelled.
  • Friction values are proxies (AISC slip coefficient) with no WTC 7 measurement; they are swept, not known.
  • The shell solver is new; it is verified against closed-form results and CalculiX for the checks listed, not for every mechanism.
  • The sweeps change one input at a time; only two combinations were run, and they disagree. The space between them is not explored.
  • The mesh and quasi-static checks fail (Section 8). Runs on two or more threads are not bit-for-bit repeatable: the combined run's events moved by up to 0.07 s between two identical runs.
  • All runs were made on one laptop (docs/compute_log.md); a half-rate run took 3.7 to 10 h.

10. Next steps

  2. Decide on the two failed checks (docs/phase1_gate.md). The gate stays closed until then.
  3. Phase 2 (NIST's computed fire temperatures) does not start before the gate passes.
